# The People Who Changed Formula 1 — editorial research brief

Status: **unpublished research plan**. No historical or linguistic approval is implied. This document does not alter the site's publication registry.

## Reader promise

A result tells you who won. A story should explain why the result became possible, what the protagonists risked, and what changed afterward. The following ten cross-domain narratives are proposed for substantive EN/UK research. They are **not completed biographies**.

| Narrative | Reader hook | Connected records | Verification focus |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Niki Lauda and James Hunt, 1976 | What is a title worth when survival is at stake? | 1976 season; Nürburgring; Fuji; Ferrari; McLaren | Medical chronology, race decisions, official final points; do not invent private thoughts |
| Ayrton Senna and Alain Prost, 1988–89 | How can the fastest partnership destroy itself? | McLaren; Suzuka; 1988 and 1989 | Dropped-score rules, collisions, stewards' findings, contemporary quotations |
| Gilles Villeneuve and René Arnoux, Dijon 1979 | Why is second place sometimes the enduring story? | French GP; Renault; Ferrari; Dijon-Prenois | First turbo victory versus on-track duel; lap-by-lap evidence |
| Ross Brawn and the 2009 rescue | How does a team facing closure become champion? | Honda; Brawn GP; Button; Barrichello; 2009 | Ownership versus constructor identity; double diffuser; race chronology |
| Sebastian Vettel at Monza 2008 | What happens when the junior team wins first? | Toro Rosso; Red Bull; Italian GP; 2008 | Weather, qualifying, official results, team chronology |
| Robert Kubica's interrupted ascent | What remains of a career after its turning point? | BMW Sauber; Canadian GP 2008; Williams 2019 | Accident chronology and return; avoid unsupported counterfactuals |
| Felipe Massa's seconds as champion | How can a victory and a title loss happen together? | Brazilian GP 2008; Ferrari; Hamilton; McLaren | Exact finishing order and official standings |
| Michael Schumacher's Ferrari transformation | How did a long title drought end? | Ferrari; Benetton; 1996–2000; Suzuka | Team personnel, technical evolution, chronology; no single-cause mythology |
| Jenson Button and the 2011 Canadian GP | How can a chaotic afternoon turn into a signature win? | McLaren; Montreal; Vettel; 2011 | Penalties, stops, race duration, late pass from authoritative race reports |
| Sergio Pérez's Sakhir comeback | What does a first win mean after years of near misses? | Racing Point; Sakhir GP 2020; Mercedes | First-lap incident, classification, chronology; distinguish team lineage |

## Narrative structure for each completed story

**Quick**: an original 60–100-word EN/UK hook and stakes, comprehensible without prior F1 knowledge.

**Explore**: 3–5 narrative acts (at least 500 original words per language for a full-length story), explaining antecedents, decisions, pivotal races, consequences, and reasonable uncertainty. Do not turn speculation into reported motivation.

**Deep Dive**: exact primary-source locators and a concise evidence ledger with claim, publisher, URL, scope, corroboration and unresolved questions. An F1DB classification verifies classified results, not radio messages, subjective emotions or overtaking maneuvers.

**Continue exploring**: validated IDs/links to relevant season, race, driver, constructor and circuit pages. Resolve against the pinned F1DB snapshot before inserting links into the application.
