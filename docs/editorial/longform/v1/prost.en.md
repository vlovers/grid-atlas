# Alain Prost: the long route to four championships

## From junior racing to Formula 1

Alain Prost's path to Formula 1 included the 1979 European Formula Three championship and a McLaren test at Paul Ricard. He joined McLaren for the 1980 season, moved to Renault for 1981–1983 and returned to McLaren in 1984. This was not one uninterrupted rise within a single team; changes of machinery and organisation shaped the sequence of his career. [Evidence: `alain-prost-junior-career`, `alain-prost-teams`]

Prost's first Formula 1 victory came at the 1981 French Grand Prix in Dijon, driving for Renault. The milestone belongs to his Renault period, before the McLaren championships for which he is also remembered. [Evidence: `alain-prost-first-victory`]

## The title that slipped away

In 1984 Prost won seven races, yet team-mate Niki Lauda took the championship by half a point. It is a concise illustration of why wins and a season's official points classification are different ways of describing performance. The season also came amid a change in racing conditions: McLaren's historical account notes the increased significance of fuel economy after mid-race refuelling was banned. [Evidence: `alain-prost-1984`, `alain-prost-context`]

Prost then won the drivers' titles in 1985 and 1986. A third followed in 1989, still with McLaren. Formula 1's retrospective characterises his approach as careful management of tyres and brakes, holding performance in reserve; that interpretation helps explain the nickname “The Professor”, but should remain attributed rather than treated as a measurement of every race. ## Senna, Ferrari and a final return

Ayrton Senna became Prost's McLaren team-mate in 1988, when the team won 15 of 16 races. Their rivalry continued after Prost moved to Ferrari for 1990–1991. The official 1988 classification puts Senna ahead on 90 counted points to Prost's 87. Neither that table nor later recollections alone resolve the contested interpretations of subsequent Suzuka incidents. [Evidence: `alain-prost-rivalry`, `alain-prost-1988-classification`]

After sitting out 1992, Prost returned with Williams in 1993. His second place at the Portuguese Grand Prix in Estoril secured his fourth championship. He had announced that weekend that he would retire at season's end, closing a career that had taken him through McLaren, Renault, Ferrari and Williams.
