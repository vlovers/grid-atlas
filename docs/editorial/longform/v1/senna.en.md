## A career measured in turning points

Ayrton Senna entered Formula 1 with Toleman in 1984. His second-place finish at Monaco that June offers an early, concrete point of reference: the official result records Alain Prost as the winner and Senna as runner-up. It is a striking result to revisit, but the classification alone does not tell the whole story of the race, or establish a driver's intentions. [Evidence: `ayrton-senna-opening`, `ayrton-senna-monaco-1984-p2`]

The next season brought a change of team and a victory. Senna joined Lotus in 1985 and won the Portuguese Grand Prix on 21 April in a Lotus-Renault. That result stands as a milestone in the record, without requiring an unsupported claim about what the win meant to every observer at the time. [Evidence: `ayrton-senna-opening`, `ayrton-senna-portugal-1985-p1`]

## The McLaren years

After three seasons at Lotus, Senna moved to McLaren for 1988. He won the drivers' championship that year and added titles in 1990 and 1991. The distinction between a championship result and a simple sum of race-result points matters especially in 1988: the official standings record Senna on 90 counted points and Prost on 87. A raw archive aggregation should not replace that classification. [Evidence: `ayrton-senna-teams`, `ayrton-senna-championships`, `ayrton-senna-1988-standings`]

Senna and Prost shared a McLaren garage before their championship rivalry extended beyond it. Formula 1 historian Gerald Donaldson describes their contests, including Suzuka in 1989 and 1990, in a retrospective account. That is useful historical interpretation, not a licence to declare either driver's motives as established fact. The contemporary record and competing accounts deserve separate examination. ## One more Monaco chapter

The official 1993 Monaco result records another Senna victory, this time with McLaren-Ford. It is a useful endpoint for a selection of documented milestones: second at Monaco in 1984, victory in Portugal in 1985, and victory at Monaco in 1993. These three results are not a substitute for a full season-by-season history. [Evidence: `ayrton-senna-monaco-1993-p1`]
