## A career shaped by speed and rivalry

Ayrton Senna became one of Formula One's defining figures across a career that ran from the mid-1980s into the early 1990s. The Brazilian driver won three World Drivers' Championships, in 1988, 1990 and 1991. His record belongs to an era when championship scoring rules, car reliability and qualifying performance shaped how results were interpreted. [senna-001; needs_review]

## The first season: Toleman, 1984

Senna entered Formula One with Toleman in 1984. The official results table records him at the Brazilian Grand Prix on 25 March, driving a Toleman Hart, with a retirement. This is a useful starting point for his Grand Prix chronology, but the project's statistics must still distinguish a race entry from a race start. [senna-002; needs_review]

## 1988: a first world championship

In 1988, Senna won the Drivers' Championship for McLaren Honda. Formula One's official final standings list Senna first on 90 championship points and teammate Alain Prost second on 87. Those figures are *championship points after the period's scoring rules*, not a straightforward sum of every result: only a driver's best 11 results counted toward the championship. The distinction matters when describing how the title was decided. [senna-004; needs_review]

## The final season

Senna's final Formula One season was 1994. He died after an accident during the San Marino Grand Prix weekend at Imola.
