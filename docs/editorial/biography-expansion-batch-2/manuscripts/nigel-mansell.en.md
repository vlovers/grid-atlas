# Nigel Mansell — the long route to number one

## An apprenticeship at Lotus

Nigel Mansell reached Formula 1 through karting and junior single-seaters, then a Lotus test in 1979. The opportunity brought a test-driver role and a championship debut at the 1980 Austrian Grand Prix. He became a full-time Lotus driver the following year. [mansell-williams] [mansell-hall] <!-- nigel-mansell-b2-early -->

His first podium came in Belgium in 1981. Further podiums followed, but his Lotus years ended without a championship win. In 1985 he moved to Williams and joined Keke Rosberg. [f1db] <!-- nigel-mansell-b2-first-podium -->

## Williams-Honda and the first wins

Mansell won the 1985 European Grand Prix at Brands Hatch and followed it with victory in South Africa. These were his first two championship wins, both in a Williams-Honda. [f1db] [mansell-williams] <!-- nigel-mansell-b2-1985 -->

The 1986 championship ended in Adelaide with Mansell suffering a rear-tyre failure. Alain Prost took the title, with Mansell second and Williams team-mate Nelson Piquet third. Mansell had won five races. [mansell-mclaren] [mansell-honda] [f1db] <!-- nigel-mansell-b2-1986 -->

## Piquet, Ferrari and the return

In 1987 Mansell won six races to Piquet’s three, yet Piquet became champion and Mansell was again runner-up. Honda’s event records show both the victories and the retirements within that rivalry. [mansell-honda] [f1db] <!-- nigel-mansell-b2-1987 -->

After a difficult 1988 season he moved to Ferrari. Victory on his debut for the team in Brazil in 1989 was followed by another win in Hungary. He spent two seasons there before returning to Williams for 1991. The Ferrari spell added notable victories but no title, while the renewed Williams partnership would bring him back into the central championship contest. [f1db] [mansell-williams] <!-- nigel-mansell-b2-ferrari -->

## The FW14B season

In 1991 Mansell won five Grands Prix but finished second to Ayrton Senna. His victories in France, Britain, Germany, Italy and Spain showed that the Williams-Renault combination could challenge McLaren over a range of circuits. [f1db] <!-- nigel-mansell-b2-1991 -->

The 1992 FW14B campaign finally changed the outcome. Mansell won the opening five races, collected nine victories across the season and secured the championship in Hungary. He finished with 108 points against team-mate Riccardo Patrese’s 56. [f1db] [mansell-williams] <!-- nigel-mansell-b2-1992 -->

## The driver’s view of demanding machinery

In a later Formula 1 interview Mansell describes how demanding the turbocharged Williams FW11B was to drive. Technical commentary alongside his recollection explains the power and packaging behind it. [mansell-car] <!-- nigel-mansell-b2-style -->

## America and the final Formula 1 return

Rather than defend the title in Formula 1, Mansell moved to Newman/Haas in American IndyCar racing and won the 1993 championship. He then returned for four Formula 1 appearances with Williams in 1994, including the French Grand Prix and the last three rounds. Victory in Australia became his final Grand Prix win, bringing the total to 31. [mansell-hall] [f1db] <!-- nigel-mansell-b2-america -->

His final Formula 1 spell came with McLaren in 1995. Cockpit-fit problems delayed his debut in the MP4/10, and he made only two race starts for the team before the partnership ended. The brief episode differed sharply from his championship year, closing a career that had passed through Lotus, Williams, Ferrari and a return to Williams before reaching McLaren. [mansell-mclaren] [f1db] <!-- nigel-mansell-b2-mclaren -->

The 1992 crown is the centre of Mansell’s Formula 1 record, but the surrounding seasons give it its meaning. There were championship challenges with Honda power, a winning Ferrari debut, a Renault-powered title and a successful move across the Atlantic. His career was a long sequence of rebuilding competitive opportunities, with the championship arriving well after his first race-winning breakthrough.
