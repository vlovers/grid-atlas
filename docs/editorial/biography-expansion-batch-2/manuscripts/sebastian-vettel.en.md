# Sebastian Vettel — four titles and the seasons beyond them

## From karting to a first Formula 1 chance

Sebastian Vettel’s route from karting in Germany to Formula 1 passed through Red Bull’s junior support and the German Formula BMW championship. He won that series in 2004 before becoming a Formula 1 test driver. His first Grand Prix start came for BMW Sauber at Indianapolis in 2007, replacing the injured Robert Kubica, and he finished eighth. [vettel-hall] [f1db] <!-- sebastian-vettel-b2-early -->

Later in 2007 he moved into a race seat with Toro Rosso. A fourth place in China preceded the much larger breakthrough at Monza in 2008, where he took pole position and won the Italian Grand Prix. His first championship victory was also Toro Rosso’s first, achieved before his promotion to Red Bull’s senior team. [f1db] <!-- sebastian-vettel-b2-toro-rosso -->

## Red Bull becomes a title contender

Vettel joined Mark Webber at Red Bull Racing in 2009. His Chinese Grand Prix victory was the constructor’s first; he added wins in Britain, Japan and Abu Dhabi and finished runner-up to Jenson Button. [f1db] <!-- sebastian-vettel-b2-2009 -->

His first world title arrived in 2010 after a contest involving Webber, Fernando Alonso and Lewis Hamilton. Vettel won the final race in Abu Dhabi and finished four points ahead of Alonso. He had not led the championship before that finale. The title therefore emerged from a late reversal, quite different from the commanding defences that followed. [f1db] <!-- sebastian-vettel-b2-2010 -->

## Four consecutive championships

In 2011 he won 11 races and took a second title in the RB7. Adrian Newey’s later account describes the exhaust-assisted downforce that gave the car strong rear stability on corner entry. [f1db] [vettel-newey] <!-- sebastian-vettel-b2-2011 -->

The 2012 title fight again went to the final race against Alonso. After first-lap contact in Brazil damaged his Red Bull, Vettel recovered to sixth while Alonso finished second. The final gap was three points. Four consecutive wins earlier in the closing part of the season had helped put Vettel back in contention before that difficult final afternoon. [vettel-hall] [f1db] <!-- sebastian-vettel-b2-2012 -->

In 2013 he won 13 races, including the final nine, and secured his fourth consecutive championship. The run began in Belgium and continued through Brazil. It was the most concentrated winning sequence of his Formula 1 career and completed Red Bull’s four-year title partnership with him, before the change to hybrid power transformed the competitive order. [f1db] <!-- sebastian-vettel-b2-2013 -->

## The conflict within the winning team

The winning years also contained conflict. At Sepang in 2013 Vettel passed Webber despite instructions to hold position and subsequently apologised. The FIA’s contemporary report records both the instruction and his response; RaceFans also reported the post-race explanation. [vettel-malaysia] [vettel-malaysia-independent] <!-- sebastian-vettel-b2-malaysia -->

In 2014 Daniel Ricciardo joined Red Bull and won three races, while Vettel won none. Newey told journalist Adam Cooper that the loss of the blown diffuser required Vettel to relearn how to extract performance. He also stressed that Vettel had been quick before that technology. [vettel-cooper] [f1db] <!-- sebastian-vettel-b2-2014 -->

## How stability became speed

Newey’s description of the RB7 gives a specific example of Vettel’s technique: he used its stable rear end to turn the car hard and early into a corner. The account concerns the relationship between one driver and a particular aerodynamic system. [vettel-newey] <!-- sebastian-vettel-b2-style -->

## Ferrari, Aston Martin and the final season

Vettel moved to Ferrari in 2015 and won in Malaysia, Hungary and Singapore during his first season. In 2017 and 2018 he finished runner-up to Hamilton, winning five races in each campaign. A Ferrari title never followed. His last Formula 1 victory came in Singapore in 2019, before he left the team after the 2020 season. [f1db] <!-- sebastian-vettel-b2-ferrari -->

Vettel spent 2021 and 2022 with Aston Martin, taking second place in Azerbaijan during the first of those seasons. In July 2022 he announced that he would retire at the end of the year. In the statement released by the team he spoke of spending more time with his family and of interests beyond Formula 1. [vettel-retirement] [f1db] <!-- sebastian-vettel-b2-aston -->

His Formula 1 career ended at Abu Dhabi in 2022 with four championships and 53 Grand Prix wins. The full chronology includes the Toro Rosso breakthrough, sustained Red Bull success and six seasons attempting to bring another title to Ferrari.
