# Emerson Fittipaldi — two crowns, then another racing life

## Building a route from Brazil

Emerson Fittipaldi’s early career combined racing with building cars. He and his brother Wilson worked on karts and Formula Vee machinery in Brazil before Emerson moved to England in 1969. Formula Ford, Formula 3 and then Formula 2 gave him a rapid route into the Lotus organisation, rather than an immediate leap from Brazil into a Grand Prix cockpit. [fittipaldi-hall] <!-- emerson-fittipaldi-b2-brazil -->

He made his championship debut at the 1970 British Grand Prix. Later that season Jochen Rindt was killed during practice at Monza, and the inexperienced Fittipaldi became central to Lotus’s remaining campaign. His first victory came at Watkins Glen in the United States, helping secure Rindt’s posthumous championship. [fittipaldi-hall] [f1db] <!-- emerson-fittipaldi-b2-1970 -->

## The Lotus championship

In 1972 Fittipaldi won in Spain, Belgium, Britain, Austria and Italy and became world champion. He was 25 when he secured the title. Jackie Stewart finished second in the standings. The five victories show how quickly the young driver had moved beyond the circumstances of his debut season to lead a championship-winning campaign of his own. [f1db] [fittipaldi-mclaren-profile] <!-- emerson-fittipaldi-b2-1972 -->

The following year Ronnie Peterson joined him at Lotus. Peterson won four races to Fittipaldi’s three, yet Fittipaldi finished ahead of him in the championship, second to Stewart. [f1db] <!-- emerson-fittipaldi-b2-peterson -->

## A second title in a different team

Fittipaldi moved to McLaren for 1974 and drove the M23. In the team’s retrospective he describes adapting to its handling during testing at Paul Ricard. He brought championship experience into a working group led by Teddy Mayer and Alastair Caldwell, while learning a different car and organisation. [fittipaldi-title] <!-- emerson-fittipaldi-b2-move -->

Victories in Brazil, Belgium and Canada carried him into a title contest with Ferrari’s Clay Regazzoni. They arrived at the United States finale level on points. Fittipaldi finished fourth at Watkins Glen and won the championship. His second title was also McLaren’s first drivers’ crown, linking his personal career to a lasting milestone in the team’s history. [f1db] [fittipaldi-title] <!-- emerson-fittipaldi-b2-regazzoni -->

He remained with McLaren in 1975, winning in Argentina and Britain and finishing runner-up to Niki Lauda. His final Formula 1 victory came at Silverstone that year. By the end of the season he had won championships with two constructors and had remained in the top two of the drivers’ standings for four consecutive years. [f1db] <!-- emerson-fittipaldi-b2-1975 -->

## A measured way of going quickly

McLaren’s account describes Fittipaldi’s smooth driving and meticulous preparation. At his first M23 test he requested more front wing while adapting to the car. For the 1974 title finale, his preparation with Gordon Coppuck included reducing rear wing to gain straight-line speed. Driving technique, balance and race planning were parts of the same approach. [fittipaldi-title] [fittipaldi-mclaren-profile] <!-- emerson-fittipaldi-b2-style -->

## Leaving the established contender

For 1976 he joined the Brazilian team associated with his brother Wilson and backed by Copersucar. The decision moved a two-time champion away from a proven front-running car into a much smaller project. There were no further Formula 1 wins, but second place in the 1978 Brazilian Grand Prix gave the enterprise a significant home result. [fittipaldi-rrdc] [f1db] <!-- emerson-fittipaldi-b2-copersucar -->

His last Formula 1 season was 1980. A third place in the United States West Grand Prix at Long Beach added a final podium before he stopped driving in the championship. [f1db] [fittipaldi-rrdc] <!-- emerson-fittipaldi-b2-1980 -->

## A second peak in America

Fittipaldi began a new career in American single-seaters in 1984. He became CART champion in 1989 and won the Indianapolis 500 in both 1989 and 1993. The Speedway’s results register those two victories independently of his Formula 1 record. [fittipaldi-rrdc] [fittipaldi-indy] <!-- emerson-fittipaldi-b2-indy -->

Two Formula 1 titles and 14 Grand Prix victories are one part of that wider career. The Lotus breakthrough, McLaren’s first drivers’ championship and the American revival each belong to a different competitive setting. Together they explain a legacy that survived the difficult family-team years and crossed beyond the category in which he first became world champion.
