# Graham Hill — a career across disciplines

## An unusually late beginning

Graham Hill reached racing by a route far removed from the modern karting ladder. The International Motorsports Hall of Fame records that he bought a road car and passed his driving test in 1953, aged 24, before trying a racing car at Brands Hatch. Work as a mechanic helped bring him into contact with Colin Chapman and Lotus. [graham-museum] <!-- graham-hill-b2-beginnings -->

His Formula 1 debut came with Lotus at Monaco in 1958. Two seasons with that constructor were followed by a move to BRM in 1960. The eventual champion therefore spent several years in the championship before winning a race. [f1db] <!-- graham-hill-b2-lotus-brm -->

## BRM’s championship driver

In 1962 Hill won in the Netherlands, Germany, Italy and South Africa and became world champion with BRM. Jim Clark was his closest challenger in the final standings. The four victories linked very different circuits, from Zandvoort to the Nürburgring, and turned the long early preparation into a complete championship campaign rather than an isolated maiden success. [f1db] [graham-lemans] <!-- graham-hill-b2-1962 -->

Monaco became the most recognisable thread in Hill’s Formula 1 results. He won there in 1963, 1964 and 1965 for BRM, then in 1968 and 1969 for Lotus. [f1db] [graham-lemans] <!-- graham-hill-b2-monaco -->

## Rivals, team-mates and a second title

The years after his first title remained highly competitive. Hill was runner-up in 1963, 1964 and 1965, with Clark taking two of those championships and John Surtees the other. This was sustained contention rather than a single peak. It also placed Hill among a generation whose leading drivers repeatedly exchanged the roles of champion, pursuer and team-mate. [f1db] <!-- graham-hill-b2-1964-rival -->

Hill returned to Lotus in 1967, joining Clark. After Clark’s death the following spring, Hill won the 1968 championship with victories in Spain, Monaco and Mexico. The second title came six years after the first and with another constructor. His final championship win followed at Monaco in 1969, completing a Formula 1 total of 14 victories. [f1db] [graham-lemans] <!-- graham-hill-b2-1968 -->

## Indianapolis and Le Mans

In 1966 Hill won the Indianapolis 500 on his first appearance in the race. The Speedway records the winning car as a Lola-Ford, entered by John Mecom. That success was separate from the Formula 1 championship: it added an American oval victory to a career already established on European road and street circuits. [graham-indy] [graham-lemans] <!-- graham-hill-b2-indy -->

At Le Mans in 1972 Hill shared a Matra with Henri Pescarolo and won the 24-hour race. Together with his Monaco and Indianapolis victories, it completed the combination commonly called motorsport’s Triple Crown. He had also won the Formula 1 world championship twice, bringing success on a street circuit, an American oval and an endurance course into the same career. [graham-lemans] [graham-lemans-drive] <!-- graham-hill-b2-lemans -->

## A craft recognised by a co-driver

Pescarolo’s recollection, published by the Le Mans organiser, singles out Hill’s driving in the rain during their 1972 victory. The account describes his choice of intermediate tyres as the competing Matra used slicks. [graham-lemans-drive] <!-- graham-hill-b2-style -->

## The difficult final seasons

A major accident at Watkins Glen in 1969 injured Hill’s legs. He returned to Formula 1, but the subsequent seasons brought no further championship victories. His record includes Lotus machinery, Brabham and then the team he established himself. [graham-hall] [f1db] <!-- graham-hill-b2-injury -->

Hill stopped driving in Formula 1 in 1975 and intended to continue running his team. On 29 November that year he died in an aircraft crash that also killed team members, including driver Tony Brise. [graham-hall] [graham-lemans] <!-- graham-hill-b2-ending -->
