# Niki Lauda — three titles, two racing careers

## Financing the climb

Niki Lauda entered racing against the expectations of his Austrian business family. Starting in a Mini, he progressed through junior single-seaters and financed the climb with loans. [lauda-hall] <!-- niki-lauda-b2-early -->

His first championship appearance came with March at the 1971 Austrian Grand Prix. BRM followed in 1973, then Ferrari in 1974. The Ferrari move changed his results quickly: he finished second in his first race for the team in Argentina and won in Spain and the Netherlands. The driver who had paid to establish himself was now winning with a leading constructor. [f1db] <!-- niki-lauda-b2-brm-ferrari -->

## Ferrari’s champion

Lauda won five races in 1975 and secured his first world championship. Emerson Fittipaldi finished second. The success ended Ferrari’s wait for a drivers’ title since John Surtees in 1964. Wins at Monaco, Zolder and Anderstorp formed the early core of a season in which Lauda turned his first year’s promise into a championship-winning partnership. [f1db] [lauda-mercedes] <!-- niki-lauda-b2-1975 -->

The 1976 German Grand Prix interrupted his title defence. Lauda survived a fire after crashing at the Nürburgring and suffered serious injuries. He returned at Monza six weeks later and finished fourth. [lauda-monza] [f1db] <!-- niki-lauda-b2-monza -->

## Fuji and a second Ferrari title

At the rain-soaked Japanese finale Lauda withdrew, judging the conditions too dangerous. Hunt finished third and won the championship by one point. Their contest is often remembered through the accident and comeback, but the ending also involved a deliberate decision about acceptable risk. Lauda returned the following year and won the 1977 title rather than ending his career with that defeat. [lauda-hall] [f1db] <!-- niki-lauda-b2-fuji -->

He left Ferrari after securing that second championship and joined Brabham for 1978. Wins in Sweden and Italy followed, but the new partnership did not produce another title. During the 1979 Canadian Grand Prix weekend he retired from driving. Formula 1 had already given him two championships; for a time his attention moved to the airline business rather than the next race. [lauda-hall] [f1db] <!-- niki-lauda-b2-brabham -->

## McLaren and a different challenge

Lauda returned with McLaren in 1982. He won at Long Beach in only his third championship race back, then added a British Grand Prix victory later in the season. The comeback developed into a sustained second career, with four seasons at McLaren. It was not simply a guest appearance by a former champion revisiting his old profession. [f1db] [lauda-mclaren] <!-- niki-lauda-b2-1982 -->

In 1984 Alain Prost became his team-mate, and the McLaren pair won 12 of the 16 races. Prost won seven, Lauda five, yet Lauda took the championship by 72 points to 71.5. At the Portuguese finale Prost won the race while Lauda finished second, exactly the combination that preserved the Austrian’s narrow advantage. [f1db] [lauda-finale] <!-- niki-lauda-b2-1984 -->

## Race preparation against qualifying speed

A Formula 1 technical account of the MP4/2 explains Lauda’s response to Prost’s qualifying speed: he concentrated practice on the race set-up. The car’s fuel-efficient turbo engine made its Sunday pace particularly effective. This gives the 1984 rivalry a technical as well as a personal setting. [lauda-setup] <!-- niki-lauda-b2-setup -->

## A last victory, then another role

His final victory came at Zandvoort in 1985, ahead of Prost. He retired from Formula 1 driving at the end of the season with 25 Grand Prix wins and three titles. They were spread across 1975, 1977 and 1984, an unusually separated sequence that reflects the interruption and reinvention inside his career rather than one continuous period of dominance. [f1db] [lauda-mclaren] <!-- niki-lauda-b2-1985 -->

Lauda continued in Formula 1 beyond the cockpit, including work with Ferrari and leadership at Jaguar. He became Mercedes’ non-executive chairman in September 2012 and helped bring Lewis Hamilton to the team for 2013. [lauda-mercedes] <!-- niki-lauda-b2-mercedes -->

Lauda died on 20 May 2019. His racing titles were separated by a departure from the sport and a return with another team. His later roles at Ferrari, Jaguar and Mercedes kept him involved in the decisions behind the cars. From driver to adviser and chairman, he remained part of Formula 1 long after his last victory.
