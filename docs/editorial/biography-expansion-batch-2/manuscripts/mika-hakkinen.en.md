# Mika Häkkinen — the long McLaren apprenticeship

## From karting to the British ladder

Mika Häkkinen progressed from Finnish karting into single-seater racing with support from Keke Rosberg, the 1982 world champion. The route included Formula Ford, the Opel Lotus Euroseries and the 1990 British Formula 3 title. He joined Lotus in Formula 1 in 1991, then spent two seasons racing the team’s cars before moving to McLaren. [hakkinen-hall] <!-- mika-hakkinen-b2-early -->

In 1993 he accepted a McLaren test and development role while Ayrton Senna and Michael Andretti began the year as the race drivers. Häkkinen replaced Andretti for the final three rounds: Portugal, Japan and Australia. The testing appointment had led back to a race seat before the end of his first year with the team. [hakkinen-mclaren] [f1db] <!-- mika-hakkinen-b2-test-role -->

## Speed before the first victory

At the 1993 Portuguese Grand Prix, his first race weekend as a McLaren driver, Häkkinen qualified ahead of Senna. A podium followed in Japan. Those results made his pace visible, but the first victory remained years away. His early McLaren career crossed changes of engine supplier and seasons in which a podium was a more realistic target than a championship. [hakkinen-hall] [f1db] <!-- mika-hakkinen-b2-portugal -->

His career nearly ended during practice for the 1995 Australian Grand Prix in Adelaide. A serious accident left him requiring emergency treatment before a long recovery. In his own later account for McLaren, Häkkinen describes the private test at Paul Ricard that helped establish he could drive competitively again. He returned for the 1996 season. [hakkinen-australia] [f1db] <!-- mika-hakkinen-b2-adelaide -->

## Jerez, Melbourne and a championship

The first championship victory finally came at Jerez in the 1997 European Grand Prix, with David Coulthard completing a McLaren one-two. Häkkinen had already spent five seasons associated with the team. The long wait helps explain why the following year was such a change in scale: the potential demonstrated through testing, qualifying and podium finishes became a sustained winning campaign. [f1db] [hakkinen-mclaren] <!-- mika-hakkinen-b2-first-win -->

The 1998 opener in Melbourne also brought a McLaren one-two, but with a contested route to the finish. Häkkinen’s first-person account records a pre-race agreement with Coulthard and the eventual exchange of positions. The episode puts their partnership in view: Coulthard was both another fast driver in the same car and a colleague whose decisions could influence the team’s result. [hakkinen-australia] <!-- mika-hakkinen-b2-melbourne -->

Häkkinen won eight of the season’s 16 races and became world champion, ahead of Michael Schumacher. Victory at Suzuka completed the campaign. The season established the central sporting rivalry of his peak years: McLaren-Mercedes against Ferrari, with Häkkinen and Schumacher repeatedly meeting at the front rather than merely being paired later by reputation. [f1db] <!-- mika-hakkinen-b2-1998 -->

## A second title and a different challenger

He defended the title in 1999, this time finishing two points ahead of Eddie Irvine. Schumacher missed part of the season after a British Grand Prix accident, and Irvine carried Ferrari’s title challenge into the Japanese finale. Häkkinen won that final race. The second championship therefore had a different shape from the first, even though the winning team and driver were unchanged. [f1db] [hakkinen-hall] <!-- mika-hakkinen-b2-1999 -->

The Schumacher contest resumed in 2000. At Spa, Häkkinen overtook him as both came upon the lapped BAR of Ricardo Zonta, choosing the opposite side of Zonta’s car. McLaren’s account and Yle’s retrospective describe the move. Häkkinen won the race, but Schumacher won the championship; the celebrated overtake did not decide the season in Häkkinen’s favour. [hakkinen-spa] [hakkinen-spa-independent] [f1db] <!-- mika-hakkinen-b2-spa -->

## What the engineer saw

In McLaren’s technical portrait, engineer Phil Prew describes Häkkinen’s preference for a responsive front end and stability on corner entry. He also recalls the driver’s ability to adapt as a car’s behaviour changed during a race. [hakkinen-style] <!-- mika-hakkinen-b2-style -->

## The sabbatical that became retirement

Häkkinen added victories in Britain and the United States in 2001. The Indianapolis win was his last in Formula 1, and the Japanese Grand Prix his final championship start. He announced a sabbatical but never returned to Formula 1 racing. His finished F1 record contains two titles and 20 Grand Prix victories, all won with McLaren. [f1db] [hakkinen-mclaren] [hakkinen-hall] <!-- mika-hakkinen-b2-2001 -->

His departure from Formula 1 was not the end of racing. From 2005 to 2007 Häkkinen competed in DTM, the touring-car championship, and won three races. That later period took him into a different kind of car and competition after the long McLaren association, extending his career beyond the single-seater results for which he is most widely remembered. [hakkinen-dtm] <!-- mika-hakkinen-b2-dtm -->

His career joins a long period of preparation to a comparatively concentrated run of victories. The recovery from Adelaide is essential to that story, but so are the testing years, the work with Coulthard and the championship contests against Ferrari.
