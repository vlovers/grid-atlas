# Detroit 1982 and Porto 1960 — 19 individually described historical bends (research only)

## Scope and exact counts

- **Detroit, original 1982 street circuit (`detroit-1`): 17 individual direction changes** from Denis Jenkinson's contemporary street-by-street account. Parent route sections: opening riverside sweep (2), Atwater (1), Woodbridge (1), hairpin exit (3), Larned transition (2), Jefferson/tramlines (4), tunnel entry/curve (2), riverfront Esses (2). The pit-entry chicane is retained as an existing **complex**, not falsely split into individually verified apexes.
- **Porto, 1958–1960 F1 Boavista layout (`porto-1`): 2 individual bends** described in Denis Jenkinson's 1960 race report: the bottleneck after the pits and the sharp left from the tramline section into the narrow streets. This is not the 2005–2013 touring-car course.
- **Dallas, original 1984 Fair Park circuit (`dallas-1`): 1 individual final corner** documented by a period race report, specifically Mansell's last-lap wall contact before the finish. The report does not identify the exact official apex number.
- **Total new research positions: 20** (17 + 2 + 1). Together with eight existing Sebring 1959 studies, the unnumbered period-bend model now has **28 entries**.
- **New FIA-numbered / independently verified apexes: 0.** `reading_order` is editorial order only; `official_turn_number`, `numbering_year` and `name` remain `null`. Exact geographic apex anchors and modern map crosswalks remain unverified.

## Evidence and the limits of each source

**Detroit:** [Denis Jenkinson, contemporary 1982 race report](https://www.motorsportmagazine.com/archive/article/july-1982/43/the-grand-prix-of-detroit/) describes the original lap sequentially: the two-part opening left, Atwater and Woodbridge right-angle turns, a tight left hairpin and right–left, Larned, Jefferson's double-left over tramlines, the tightening left and tunnel right, and the riverfront S-bend. [RacingCircuits Detroit history](https://www.racingcircuits.info/north-america/usa/detroit.html) is an external comparison for period layouts, **not** a certified numbered 1982 FIA diagram. [Official Formula 1 Detroit 1982 classification](https://www.formula1.com/en/results/1982/races/443/detroit/race-result) supports the race/date/results only, not bend positions. The report's S-bend description does **not** independently establish each component's handedness; the EN/UK copy deliberately avoids assigning it.

**Porto:** [Denis Jenkinson, contemporary 1960 race report](https://www.motorsportmagazine.com/archive/article/september-1960/14/portuguese-grand-prix-5/) places Brabham's opening move at the bottleneck after the pits, and the troublesome sharp left where the tramline approach enters the narrow twisty section. The latter was relevant to Brabham's early wide line and Surtees's later braking error after a fuel leak. [STATS F1 circuit page](https://www.statsf1.com/en/circuit-porto.aspx) provides general historical course context, **not** an FIA-numbered corner diagram. [Official Formula 1 Portugal 1960 classification](https://www.formula1.com/en/results/1960/races/185/portugal/race-result) confirms the event and results, not the geometric mapping. Do not transplant modern Porto corner-name lists onto this F1 layout.

**Dallas:** [Alan Henry, 1984 race report](https://www.motorsportmagazine.com/archive/article/august-1984/22/dallas-grand-prix/) explicitly identifies the final corner before the finish where Mansell's Lotus lost drive. [RacingCircuits Dallas](https://www.racingcircuits.info/north-america/usa/dallas.html) is historical comparison only. [Official Formula 1 Dallas 1984 classification](https://www.formula1.com/en/results/1984/races/474/dallas/race-result) supports the race and classification, not a corner number. The report's other reference to an unspecified chicane is **not** promoted to a separate numbered apex.
