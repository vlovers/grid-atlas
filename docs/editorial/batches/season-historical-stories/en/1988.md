# 1988: sixteen races, two trajectories

**RESEARCH DRAFT · not for publication**

Sixteen Grands Prix, three winning drivers and two McLaren teammates at the top of the standings. The sequence of winners traces a championship without claiming to explain mechanical causes, strategy or intentions.

## The season's arc

The opening stretch runs from Brazil through Mexico, followed by Canada through Belgium. Gerhard Berger won in Italy, before the final four rounds brought the calendar to a close. Each event must be read through its own classification.

## Championship classification

The final standings list Ayrton Senna on 90 points and Alain Prost on 87. Do not substitute a straight sum of all race points: the era's championship rules affected which results counted.

## Three scenes for deeper investigation

1. **Monaco** — Prost won while Senna was not classified; the result alone does not explain why. [Classification](https://www.formula1.com/en/results/1988/races/529/monaco/race-result)
2. **Italy** — Berger won; Senna's qualifying result and the race winner are distinct evidence. [Results](https://www.formula1.com/en/results/1988/races) · [Qualifying](https://www.formula1.com/en/results/1988/races/538/italy/qualifying)
3. **Japan** — Senna first, Prost second; grid positions and any overtaking narrative require separate evidence. [Classification](https://www.formula1.com/en/results/1988/races/541/japan/race-result)
